# ciderui: its design

ciderui is apple.com's look for websites: a Tailwind CSS v4 theme on a small token contract, apple.com's parts as CSS classes (`cui-btn`, `cui-field`, `cui-globalnav`, …), and a Go module that writes their markup as gomponents nodes and carries the stylesheets and fonts. It is vpkit's architecture (`../vpkit`, `../vpkit-zola`) with apple.com where vpkit has VitePress. This holds what is decided and why, which no file of code can say. What a value is is in the file that holds it (a stylesheet, tokens.css), where it was read in the check that holds it (a test); this does not copy either.

*Measured* means read off a live page or a browser, recorded in `reference/` with its probe, page, browser and date; *stated* is the owner's; *guessed* is nobody's measurement.

## Who it is for

The owner's websites, and nobody else's (stated); the repository is public (stated, 2026-11-04: 「公開」), with no compatibility kept for anyone else (decision 1). The sites are in English or Traditional Chinese (stated; the store, totality, is zh-Hant), and they are both kinds of apple.com: marketing pages (a product, its story, its tiles) and the store (product cards, options to choose, a bag, sign-in and other forms) (stated), and crashcart's console (decision 19). They render their pages in Go with gomponents and link the stylesheet the module serves (decisions 26, 34). ciderui has no documentation site and draws no documentation (decision 29). With no consumer outside, a component takes the best shape and the sites follow it (decision 1).

## The standard

**apple.com, measured, for how a part looks** (decision 2). apple.com's own pages are the original, and they can be read: `reference/apple.com/` holds the computed styles of its pages (listed in its MEASURED.md) at 1600, 1200, 900 and 390px. apple.com's CSS is neither licensed nor versioned (its files carry cache hashes), so none of it is copied here and there is no upstream to re-sync: what is recorded is what elements compute to, dated, and a re-measure is a deliberate act that changes the record and the checks that read it.

**The HIG and the quietest page, for whether a part or an effect is there** (decision 25). Agents write the markup; people read the page, and nothing on it competes with the content for their attention. Where apple.com draws an effect that only draws the eye, the effect is left out, or kept to what the HIG asks of it: feedback for an action, a change of state made clear.

For what apple.com's pages are not, Apple's other sites are the standard, each read as apple.com's are:

- **The console** (decision 19): App Store Connect's newer look, its analytics pages' (stated, 2026-10-24: 「我們因為 ciderui沒有而自己做的元件很醜很不apple style，應該在ciderui新增這些元件，然後真的找apple的網站去量」), read in the owner's browser with their account (`reference/asc/`, light only, at 1920px; stated, 2026-10-23: 「App Store Connect 要量，可以用我的帳號」). That look is a design system of tokens its pages declare (`--tb-…`), recorded as its page computes them (`reference/asc/results/tokens.json`), with what its pages draw (`analytics*`, `hover-*`). Beside it, also read in the owner's browser: Apple Account (`reference/apple-account/`), Feedback Assistant (`reference/feedback-assistant/`), the Apple Developer account (`reference/developer-account/`, its appearance set light), Apple Developer Forums' post lists (`reference/developer-forums/`, signed out, 2026-10-28), and Apple Developer's System Status and releases list (`reference/apple.com/`'s `system-status*`, `releases`). The console is light (stated, 2026-10-23: 「淺色」), its dark derived (decision 18). CloudKit Console is left out: its classes are App Store Connect's toolkit's, and its paths carry the team's ID, which the reader does not mask.
- **DocC's listing**, at the console's size (`cui-console-listing`): developer.apple.com's documentation (`reference/apple.com/`'s `docc`). support.apple.com's articles (`support*`) are measured and used by no part.
- **The console's video player** (decision 28): tv.apple.com's web player, a trailer's (`reference/apple.com/`'s `tv-player*`, read in Google Chrome: Playwright's Chromium cannot play it).

What the measurements say about apple.com, and so about ciderui:

- **Dark is a section's.** None of the stylesheets its pages load reads `prefers-color-scheme`; a `.theme-dark` section is dark on a light page, a product's page dark as a whole. ciderui follows: dark is `color-scheme: dark` on any element (rule 11).
- **The type is the look, and it is responsive per class.** One class steps its size at a breakpoint by itself (`t-headline` is 48px from 735px, 32px below). Each size has one line height and one letter-spacing.
- **apple.com tunes page by page.** The marketing pages' `typography-*` classes are defined by each page for itself, and the same size is tuned two ways (32px at +0.002em on the home page, +0.004em on the store). ciderui curates one system from them (the type, below).
- **The palette is small**: a dozen colors and their alphas.
- **The breakpoints are apple.com's own**: 735px and 1069px for the content, 834px for the global nav, measured by stepping a headline and the nav across each boundary.

## What a component is

A component is a class, or a block of a few classes, that gives an element the look of one of apple.com's parts (or the console's), and nothing else: no JavaScript, no page layout, no behavior beyond what CSS reads off the element's own state. The page opens the dialog and checks the option; the component shows the state the page set. Behavior comes from HTML's own elements where it exists: `<dialog>` for a sheet, `<details>` for an accordion and a menu, `popover` for a flyout. Where its markup is more than a class on one element, the Go module writes it.

## Rules

Every component follows these. The numbers are cited across the repository; they do not change.

1. **One component, one directory, in its set.** `components/<set>/<name>/<name>.css`, the set `site` (apple.com's parts), or `console` (`cui-console-*`, decision 19), each set's entry (`site.css`, `console.css`) importing `index.css` and then every part of it by name order, in Tailwind's `components` layer, so a utility on the same element always wins (`class="cui-btn px-8"`); `examples/<id>.html`, the canonical markup, which the Go module's tests render and the cases measure; `<name>.test.mjs`, its proof; `golden.json`, its recorded values. Nothing is unlayered; nothing uses `!important`.

2. **The class says what it is; what varies is an attribute.** The block is `cui-<name>`, a part `cui-<name>-<part>`. A mutually exclusive axis is `data-variant` (`"secondary"`, `"secondary-neutral"`), `data-size` (`sm`, `lg`), `data-color` or `data-shape` (a console button's `capsule`, the toolkit's controlShape); an orthogonal switch is a class; a state is what the markup carries anyway: an ARIA attribute (`aria-current`, `aria-invalid`, `aria-expanded`), a control's own state (`checked`, `disabled`, read as `:checked` and `:disabled`), `[open]`, or `data-state` where none says it. A variant's rule is `.cui-<name>[data-variant="y"]`, so a state one attribute more wins over every variant whatever the order.

3. **Component-local variables.** The block sets its own `--cui-<name>-*` from the contract; a variant overrides the locals and nothing else, so a component nested in another keeps its colors and recoloring one block is one rule on it.

4. **Rules for what is inside sit behind `:where()`**, adding no specificity, so a component placed inside another keeps its own look.

5. **Hover is `@media (hover: hover) { &:hover }`**, so a tapped element does not keep its hover. Here ciderui departs from apple.com, whose hover stays on a touch screen nearly everywhere (`touch-*:*`, an emulated one; tapped, `tap-*:*`), its store's card's lift alone off (`html.no-touch`, its script's): `test/rules.mjs` holds every `:hover` in the kit behind the query. A tapped element matches `:hover` after the tap: a hover rule of its own shows, one behind the query does not, in Safari on the iOS simulator (XCUITest's taps) and in Chrome and Chromium (the DevTools protocol's touch events) (`reference/tap/`).

6. **Size and placement are the page's**: no width, no margin, no position, with three kinds of exception, each named by a comment beside its declaration: the type, whose classes carry their small-screen step; the sizes apple.com fixes (a control's height, a field's 56px); and a containing block a component's own parts are placed in, which places nothing itself (the global nav's `position: relative`, which a site's `sticky` replaces, and its `z-index`, apple.com's 9999). Utilities remove them.

7. **Tokens: only the contract's.** A component reads tokens.css's colors and its non-color tokens and no literal color; the token test holds every stylesheet to it.

8. **apple.com's control sizes, not one height.** apple.com's pill comes in four heights, measured; a site uses two (`button.test.mjs` holds each to its probe, the default the store's shared button styles'). A field is the largest (decision 7).

9. **One focus ring.** `base.css` draws `:focus-visible` on every element, apple.com's on every control it was read on; a text field shows its accent edge in its place (apple.com's field draws no ring for a click, `retail:field.input`, and its script's ring for the keyboard alone, which CSS cannot tell apart on a text field). A component draws no ring of its own; it restates the ring only to move it or, a field, to set it aside.

10. **Motion is apple.com's, where decision 25 keeps it, through the tokens.** No pointer's color change transitions: a button's (apple.com's 0s, every button measured) or any other part's (decision 32). A part that moves moves at apple.com's durations and curves, measured from its recorded animations (`home:menu.open`, `flyout:nav`), its test's checks naming them. `base.css` neutralizes every transition and animation under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

11. **Dark comes through the tokens, set on any element.** Every color is a `light-dark()` pair declared once on `:root`; `cui-dark` on an element sets `color-scheme: dark` and every pair inside it resolves dark, `cui-light` inside it light again (measured in Safari 27.0.1, Chrome 155, Chromium 151 and the iOS 26.5 simulator, `reference/scheme/`). On `<html>` it darkens the whole page. apple.com's dark product page also clears its global nav over the hero (`product:nav`), which is the global nav's own treatment, not the scheme's. The site set has no automatic dark mode: apple.com has none. The console follows the system (decision 35).

12. **Icons are inline SVG**, from the Go module's private `go/internal/glyph`, in `currentColor`. apple.com draws its chevrons and plus signs with its own icon font (`SF Pro Icons`), which is Apple's; ciderui draws them as SVG from the numbers of apple.com's glyphs (`reference/icons/`), so a page needs no font and no `data:` image for them (vpkit's decision 13). Every icon apple.com draws none of is Lucide's (decision 17), and never one in a component's place where apple.com draws its own.

13. **Proof.** Each check in a component's test names its source (test/harness.mjs's keys): `apple` (equal to what `reference/apple.com/results/` records for the probe it names, at the width it names: apple.com's pages and developer.apple.com's), `asc` (equal to what App Store Connect's page drew, `reference/asc/results/`, at every width), `tb` (equal to the App Store Connect toolkit's token it names, as the page computes it beside the element) or `golden` (equal to `golden.json`: a part with no instance to measure, or a departure, with the check's why). No list of known differences: every departure is a golden check with its reason beside it. What every part keeps (rules 5, 9, 10, the letter-spacing) `test/rules.mjs` reads off every example, so a new part has no line to forget. What no check names, `test/pictures.mjs` holds: a hash of every example's picture at every width, at rest; a state, a popover or a touch screen a picture at rest cannot see stays a check.

14. **Ships complete.** The stylesheet, with no header: it does not restate what Tailwind's preflight gives (`dist/<set>.css` carries it, decision 34), though what it sets against the page around it stays; its markup is its examples', its variants its rules, its sources its test's, and a comment in it only what none of those can say; its examples, its test with its golden values, and its Go component where its markup is more than one element. One commit per component, and one per step of any change, each with `npm test` green.

## Comments

Code, names, types and checks carry what they can; a comment carries only what none of them can, and every comment that is not an exported declaration's own godoc carries one tag naming its kind, so one grep finds every trap, invariant and outside fact in the repository (`comments_test.go` holds every file to this):

- `@invariant:` a contract RSI must not break: a boundary, a state, an ordering or a coupling the types do not enforce.
- `@trap:` a solution that failed here, or why what looks wrong is right: so the next change does not restore the failure.
- `@external:` a limit or side-effect of an outside system (a browser, apple.com, the build, the harness), or an environment assumption.

Godoc on an exported symbol is the interface itself and carries no tag. `go/lucide/lucide.go` is generated, and `scripts/build-lucide.mjs`, whose comment lines are the file's doc, writes it: edit the script, never the file; both are excepted. An Example's `Output:` comment is the test framework's assertion, not a comment.

## The token contract

`tokens.css` is the contract: the colors every component reads, each one `light-dark()` pair, light first, or one value where both sides are the same; what one is for, where its name does not say, is a comment beside it.

- **apple.com's slots, `--cui-c-*`**: apple.com's values, measured, both sides. Where one falls short, it is fitted (decision 6). A slot enters with the first component that reads it, its dark side measured then, so no stylesheet reads a guess. A field and the footer are light on a dark page too, as apple.com's are: their slots have one value.
- **The console's slots, `--cui-c-console-*`**: App Store Connect's toolkit's color roles, one slot per role a part reads, named by the role (`--cui-c-console-neutral-label-primary` is `--tb-color-role-neutral-label-primary`). Light is the role as its page computes it; dark is derived (decision 18); short ones fitted (decision 21). A console part reads these alone (the dialog's backdrop, `--cui-c-scrim`, and the player's video, `--cui-c-video` and `--cui-c-on-video*`, are the exceptions: the toolkit names none; decision 28).

The palette is fixed: no site redefines a color, and there are no themes (decision 15).

Not themed, each measured: in tokens.css, the focus ring (rule 9), the shadows (apple.com's card's, the toolkit's), the font stacks and the letter-spacing per size (the type); in the stylesheet of the part that draws them, the radii, the control sizes (rule 8), the durations and curves (rule 10), the material (a curtain and a scrim dim alone, decision 32) and the content widths.

## The type

**The stack** is tokens.css's `--cui-font`. SF cannot be shipped; on Apple's platforms the first two names are SF (`-apple-system` in Safari, `BlinkMacSystemFont` in Chrome; not `system-ui`: it is a font everywhere, so Inter after it would never be drawn). Elsewhere neither name is a font and the text is Inter, the kit's (`fonts.css`, Inter 4.001 with its `opsz` axis), fetched only there: in Safari 27.0.1, Chrome 155 and iOS 26.5's Safari the stack draws the system font and not one of Inter's faces is fetched; in Chromium on Linux it draws Inter with its Latin face alone (measured, `reference/stack/`; decisions 3, 4). The four names after Arial draw what Inter and Arial lack, the store's zh-Hant, so it needs no override of its own; a Latin page does not reach them (Chromium 151 on Linux read the same before and after, 2026-11-06; which of them draws Chinese is not measured). Code's stack, `--cui-font-mono`, is SF Mono on Apple's platforms and the platform's own elsewhere, no font shipped.

**The ladder** is the store's `t-` classes (measured: one coherent ladder, where the marketing pages define theirs page by page), named `cui-<name>` (`cui-headline`, `cui-eyebrow`, `cui-body`, `cui-caption`, …), each with one step at 735px; `cui-body` is ciderui's (the store defines no `t-body`).

**The letter-spacing** is per size, and it is not apple.com's numbers: apple.com sets them on its SF Pro webfonts, which carry no tracking, while the system font carries Apple's size-specific tracking itself, the same in Safari and Chrome on macOS and in Safari on iOS; apple.com's values on the system font would track it twice. ciderui's are apple.com's minus the system font's own, measured size by size (`reference/tracking/cui-tracking.json`, the values in tokens.css). Where apple.com tunes a size two ways, the store's is taken. CSS has no letter-spacing per font of a stack, so Inter takes SF's values and runs wide of apple.com's text (decision 3).

**Traditional Chinese** is apple.com/tw's (`store-tw`, `buy-tw`, `home-tw`), set by the page's `lang` with no class of its own: tokens.css's `[lang|="zh"]` holds the letter-spacing, the system's Chinese face carrying no tracking at any size (1em an ideograph, in Chromium on macOS, 2026-11-06); a `:where(:lang(zh))` in a class or part holds a taller line height, its specificity none, so a utility still wins. The option's title, the accordion's answer and the phone menu's links are read there; the field, the ribbon and the footer's line heights are not, nor a nested `lang="en"`, which inherits the tracking. The console's type is not set apart: App Store Connect was not read in Chinese.

**The classes are Tailwind utilities** (`type.css`, `@utility`), not component classes: measured in Tailwind 4.3.3 and Chromium 151, a class holds its own step at 735px, a variant composes with it (`cui-headline lg:cui-headline-elevated`), and a plain utility on the same element wins at every width (`cui-headline text-[20px]`), as rule 1 wants of a component.

## The page

**Breakpoints.** theme.css replaces Tailwind's with apple.com's, in pixels as apple.com's media queries are (its stylesheets name `xl`'s width; nothing measured changes there) (decision 9). A component's step and a page's `md:` switch at the same width. A set or a part may step at its own widths inside itself: the global nav at 834px, the console at 640px and 1024px (decision 20).

**Sections** are the page's own: no part draws one (decision 33). A section's ground is `bg-page` or `bg-alt`, from a site's own utilities, or `cui-dark`.

## What is not built or measured

Not built: anything of Apple's developer documentation but the console's listing (decision 29).

Not measured: the store's checkout pages (the agent's recommendation, which the owner took, 「都按照你的推薦」, 2026-10-21): they are not reachable without a bag, a headless browser's Add to Bag is refused (a 541), and getting past that would be getting past apple.com's bot protection; their form parts were read on the gift card's, the trade-in's and the feedback form's pages. Nor a phone's own screen: the taps are the iOS simulator's and Chromium's (`reference/tap/`); apple.com's pages are tapped in Chromium alone (`tap-*:*`). When a part is built, its probes are added where the reference's are not enough.

## The Go module

- **The components**, in their set's package (`site`, `console`, under `go/`), for the parts whose markup is more than a class on one element, and the buttons (`site.Btn`, `console.ConsoleBtn`), a page's most written control, whose variants a typo in a class string would lose until a test rendered that page; a link in running text is a class string on the page's own element; so is a console table with sort links or a selection column (`console.ConsoleTable` writes every other, each cell named by its column; its scroll box, the keyboard's to reach, is the page's own markup — the example sticky.html — that no table which does not overflow carries a tab stop of its own). A type is named by its class without `cui-`, in CamelCase (`cui-field` is `site.Field`, `cui-console-badge` `console.ConsoleBadge`): the set's prefix stays though the package names it, so one grep finds the class, the type and a site's uses. Where a part writes one piece of a larger block the type names that piece (`site.OptionTile`, `console.ConsoleCheckboxBox`, `console.ConsoleListItem`).
- **Each component is a struct a call writes every field of**: `go tool exhaustruct` fails a literal of the module's types that leaves one out, in this repository's gate and in a site's, so a field added stops every call that does not set it. A variant is a struct of one unexported field, its values package variables so a call cannot invent one, its zero value the default (the variant writing no attribute), named like the other values, `{value: ""}` (`console.ConsoleEmptySizeSmall`), or, where no default fits, a panic when rendered; a two-valued axis is one bool (`Btn.Block`); an absent text is `""`, an absent node nil. A part writes no words of its own: each is a field that panics when empty, only where the part draws it (`GlobalNav.CloseAriaLabel`). What repeats in a part is `Items`, the part writing its `<li>`, ids and dots so they cannot disagree. Attributes on a control (`disabled`, `required`) go in its `Attrs`. A text the page shows is `Label`; one only assistive technology reads is `AriaLabel`, or the part's name before it where it labels a part (`TabsAriaLabel`, `CloseAriaLabel`, `LogoAriaLabel`), so one grep finds them all. A precondition the type cannot hold is a panic naming the package's type.
- **The markup and the CSS cannot part**: `examples/<id>.html` is the one copy of a part's markup; its Go component's test renders equal to it.
- **`go/internal/glyph`**: the components' own icons (rule 12), private, each with the box of its one place, so a site cannot pick `glyph.Menu` (18 by 18) where it means `lucide.Menu` (1em). **`lucide`**: every Lucide icon, named by Lucide's name in Go's capitals (`shopping-bag` is `lucide.ShoppingBag`), 1em square (decision 17), and `lucide.Icon`, the type of a field or parameter that holds one. `go/lucide/lucide.go` is generated: edit `scripts/build-lucide.mjs`, never the file.
- **The stylesheets and fonts** are embedded in the root package, so a site's dependency at a commit is the one pin for its markup and its CSS (decision 13). `ciderui.Assets` serves `dist/`, built and held fresh by `scripts/build-css.mjs`; `site.Page` and `console.ConsolePage` write the document that links it (decision 34). `go tool ciderui-css <dir>` writes what a site's own utilities' stylesheet imports.
- **`ciderui.CheckMarkup`**: a site's tests call it on every page they render, since a misspelt class or `data-*` value draws the default with no error, and a misspelt `aria-*` state is wrong for assistive technology. What it refuses is `markup_test.go`'s.

## How a component is proven

`npm test` is the gate. A picture changed on purpose is re-recorded with `UPDATE_GOLDEN=1 node scripts/docker.mjs node test/pictures.mjs`; a failed run leaves the new picture in `test/pictures-failed`. What the measurements taught the harness, which it keeps:

- **Linux's hinting.** Chromium on Linux hints every glyph to a whole-pixel advance unless launched with `--font-render-hinting=none`; with it, its text widths are the Mac's within 0.00006em a character (measured, `reference/tracking/`). Every run passes the flag.
- **The fonts differ between the two sides.** apple.com's values were read with its SF Pro webfonts; ciderui's cases render on Linux in Inter, fonts.css's, inlined into each case's page. So an `apple` check compares what does not depend on the text's width (the type's own values, colors, borders, radii, padding, heights where the line height fixes them), and a box that depends on the text's width is a `golden` check.
- **Transitions.** A state is forced with transitions off, and styles are flushed before they return, or a later read lands in flight.
- **States the protocol cannot force.** `:checked` is not a pseudo-class DevTools forces, so a checked state is proven by an example with `checked` in its markup, against the probe that measured apple.com's classes on a checked copy (`buy:option.checked`); such a probe says so in its name and its spec.
- **What only Safari or iOS can answer** (the system font's tracking, a section's color scheme, the stack's fetching, the nav's popover menu) is measured on macOS (`reference/browsers.mjs`'s `safari`, `ios`, `chrome`), not assumed from Chromium: Safari on air, a Mac nobody sits at (`reference/air.mjs`), iOS's simulator and Chrome on a Mac with Xcode.
- **States a click makes.** `:popover-open` is not forced either: a case clicks the element that makes it (`click`), then moves the pointer off, which a click leaves over what it clicked, hovered; it presses keys (`press`: Tab, Escape) and reads where the focus is (`focused`); `wait: 0` reads its motion's start (`@starting-style` through the compiled stylesheet).
- **A scroller's snapping.** `scroll` scrolls a case's scroller to a place before its checks, at every width: between two snap positions it comes to rest at one, at its end its last item as far in as its first.
- **What the pointer finds.** A control under another layer, or a link laid over a box, has no box of its own a check can read: `hit` reads what the pointer finds at five points of the box, a tenth in from each corner and its middle.

## Browsers

The kit needs `light-dark()` (vpkit's floor: Chrome 123, Safari 17.5, Firefox 120, stated there). Beyond it, each measured in the browsers below:

- the global nav's menu: `popover`, `:has()` and `@starting-style` (`reference/popover/`); its `user-select: none`: Safari's `-webkit-user-select` (`reference/select/`, by XCUITest's keyboard on the iPhone simulator). Its search and flyouts, gone by decision 36, were read in `reference/search/` and `reference/flyout/`;
- a dialog: `<dialog>` and the `command` and `commandfor` attributes, to open with no script (`reference/dialog/`; a browser without them opens it by the page's `showModal()`);
- a tap opens the menu and leaves no hover behind the query (`reference/tap/`, on a touch screen: not Safari on the Mac);
- forced colors (a high-contrast theme): the option shows its state (`reference/forced/`: Chromium's emulation, light and dark, and Firefox's override; WebKit has no forced colors), and so do the console's current tab, page and selected row, its rules and hairlines and the sidebar's sheet (their tests' forced-colors cases).

ciderui's claims are measured in Safari 27.0.1 on macOS 27.0.1, Safari 26.5 on the iOS 26.5 simulator (an iPhone's; an iPad's too for the flyouts), Chrome 155 on macOS (156 for the flyouts), Chromium 151 on Linux, and Playwright's Firefox 153 on Linux (2026-10-22: each claim holds; the tracking and the stack Inter's, Linux having no SF). Not in Firefox on macOS (Playwright's does not run on the Mac over SSH; the body's `-moz-osx-font-smoothing: grayscale`, apple.com's, is for it, unmeasured), not on a real iPhone or iPad, not on Windows or Android.

The components' values are read in Chromium (`test/`). Read once in Firefox 153 (2026-10-22, by a harness outside the repository that puts each state on by real input), they differed in how a value reads, not in what is drawn: an SVG path's box (`getBoundingClientRect()` holds its stroke in Firefox), a text field's line-height (Firefox reports at least its font's `normal`; the line-height of a field of fixed height moves its text in neither), a grid's tracks (serialized evenly; the items' boxes within 0.011px); and in one thing a user meets: after a click on an empty part of the local nav's bar, Firefox starts Tab from the caret the click left in the title, past the title's link, where Chromium starts at the title (apple.com's local nav, read 2026-10-21, would do the same).

## Decisions

Numbered as cited across the repository; a new one takes the next number.

1. **The sites do not bind a component's shape.** Every consumer is the owner's; no compatibility is kept.
2. **apple.com, measured, is the standard for how a part looks.** Its live pages' computed values, recorded with their probes and dates; its CSS never copied. Whether a part or an effect is there is decision 25's.
3. **SF exact, Inter near.** The letter-spacing is fitted to the system font (within 0.0006em a character of apple.com's text at all 14 sizes in Safari and Chrome on macOS and Safari on iOS); Inter, where the system font is not SF, takes the same values and runs wide, by up to 0.0243em a character at 400 (21px), about 21px over a 60-character line (measured, `reference/tracking/`) (stated: the owner chose Inter over the platform's own font and over apple.com's Helvetica and Arial).
4. **The stack names the system font by its two Apple-only names**, so Inter is drawn, and fetched, only where SF is not. Traditional Chinese's fonts follow Arial, for the store.
5. **Dark is set on any element** (rule 11); no automatic dark mode in the site set (the console's: decision 35).
6. **The palette is apple.com's, fitted where it falls short**: apple.com's dark secondary text (`#6e6e73`) is 4.1:1 on black and its dark error (`#e30000`) 4.3:1, so tokens.css takes the next gray and Apple's dark red; the token test holds the minimums.
